name: Hero identity direction
description: Owner selected an interlocking MDR monogram; soften lettering and explore gentle color/pattern motion instead of the AI-looking film.
---

Start over with the hero artwork direction rather than refining the city-street film, tablet, package, or sculpture.

**Why:** The owner said the top image should not immediately suggest “AI,” which takes attention away from the studio's design work and can put prospective clients off.

**How to apply:** Lead with the studio's design identity rather than generated narrative imagery. Do not assume the old film or drafting motion remains the approved direction.

The owner selected the Interlocking Monogram direction from the Runway-assisted logo studies and asked to “soften up the lettering and make the lettering change pattern or color or both somehow.” They rejected the traditional serif treatment as too much like Times New Roman and requested something “clean and impactful,” but not whimsical. After seeing the geometric sans-serif refinement, they asked for typography “somewhere between where we were before and this.” The logo should occupy less space than the former hero image.

**Why:** This was the owner's explicit selection and refinement request after reviewing the three logo concepts.

**How to apply:** Keep the interlocking initials and balance the earlier elegance with the latest clarity: consider open curves, modest stroke variation and subtle flaring rather than either pronounced traditional serifs or heavy geometric construction. Do not treat pure sans-serif as an approved requirement. Keep the studio name readable and the motion restrained.

The owner approved using the latest balanced monogram on the actual homepage for now and wants to review it later.

**Why:** They said “Use the most recent for now, I'll want to review it later” and then “go aheaed and change the homepage.”

**How to apply:** Treat the balanced mark as the provisional homepage identity, not a final, permanent logo approval. Preserve the review concepts rather than deleting them.

The owner declined production approval of the balanced monogram and requested “More overlap, simpler lines,” then asked to see three options.

**Why:** Provisional homepage use does not mean the letterforms are approved for final production artwork.

**How to apply:** Get explicit approval of a revised direction before final exports; leave the homepage unchanged during logo asset review.

The owner selected “3 — Layered Monoline” from the three overlap revisions for further refinement.

**Why:** This was their explicit choice after comparing Shared Stem, Compact Weave and Layered Monoline.

**How to apply:** Continue the thin, rounded, overlapping monoline direction. Selection for refinement is not approval of subsequent letterform changes or final production exports.

The owner subsequently approved the refined Layered Monoline for production files, including its shared M/D vertical, deeper R overlap and the “MDR Design” name shown on the refinement board.

**Why:** They explicitly selected “Approve—prepare the production files” after seeing the revised letterforms and light/dark, small-size and before/after samples.

**How to apply:** Use the approved refinement as the logo production reference, not the earlier balanced raster or the unrefined three-option study. Asset preparation alone did not authorize replacing the provisional homepage artwork.

The owner subsequently authorized homepage integration: “Yes, update the homepage.”

**Why:** They explicitly approved replacing the provisional logo with the approved Layered Monoline.

**How to apply:** Use the refined Layered Monoline on the homepage, preserving restrained color/pattern motion and reduced-motion behavior. This approval does not extend to browser icons or sharing previews.

The owner expressed concern that the R sits in front of the D and selected “Put the D in front of the R” for a revision preview.

**Why:** They said they did not like the R being in front of the D.

**How to apply:** Preserve the other letterforms when changing the crossing order.

After seeing the comparison, the owner approved homepage use: “Yes, use the D-in-front revision.”

**Why:** The owner explicitly approved the reviewed D-over-R treatment for the homepage.

**How to apply:** Use D-over-R crossings on the homepage, with the other letterforms and motion unchanged. The original stored production kit is historical; this does not authorize changing browser icons or sharing previews.

The owner wants the shifting backgrounds to cover more colors with visibly richer depth, not just near-black variations.

**Why:** They repeated the request to expand the background color effect and make its color depth more robust.

**How to apply:** Retain smooth, saturated dark color transitions and readable foregrounds; do not reduce the effect back to barely distinguishable slate hues or change the logo to achieve it.

The owner requested different, complementary colors for the logo letter borders and interiors, cycling through shades.

**Why:** They want both parts to “pop and rotate different shades.”

**How to apply:** Preserve the approved D-in-front geometry while treating the letter edges and interiors as separate coordinated colors. Color rotation means shade cycling, not spinning the logo; retain pause and reduced-motion behavior.
