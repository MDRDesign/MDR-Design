Start over with the hero artwork direction rather than refining the city-street film, tablet, package, or sculpture.

**Why:** The owner said the top image should not immediately suggest “AI,” which takes attention away from the studio's design work and can put prospective clients off.

**How to apply:** Lead with the studio's design identity rather than generated narrative imagery. Do not assume the old film or drafting motion remains the approved direction.

The owner selected the Interlocking Monogram direction from the Runway-assisted logo studies and asked to “soften up the lettering and make the lettering change pattern or color or both somehow.” They subsequently rejected the traditional serif treatment as too much like Times New Roman and requested something “clean and impactful,” but not whimsical. The logo should occupy less space than the former hero image.

**Why:** This was the owner's explicit selection and refinement request after reviewing the three logo concepts.

**How to apply:** Refine the interlocking initials with clean, non-whimsical sans-serif letterforms rather than restarting wordmark or abstract-symbol exploration. Keep the studio name readable and the motion restrained. Do not return to traditional serif monograms. The direction is selected, but the refined artwork is not yet approved as a final logo.
